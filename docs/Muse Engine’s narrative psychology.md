**Muse Engine’s narrative psychology** is a deliberate inversion of how most writing tools (and most writers) work.

It treats story not primarily as plot events, but as a **psychic structure** that must be held, tested, broken, and rebuilt. The engine refuses to generate prose. Instead it forces the writer to sit inside the meaning of what they have already decided.

### 1. Core Psychological Premise

Writers usually know *what happens next* before they know *what it means*.  
Muse Engine reverses the order:

> Meaning first → action second.

By asking about color, light, thought, and symbol *before* the writer advances the plot, the tool tries to prevent two common failures:

- Flat, purely external action
- Structure that drifts because the writer is chasing events rather than psychological pressure

The engine assumes that every significant decision already contains latent symbolic content. Its job is to surface that content and make the writer conscious of it.

### 2. The Double-Climax Pyramid (the structural psyche)

The nine-stage pyramid is the engine’s central psychological model. It is not the standard three-act structure. It is closer to a **false victory → collapse → true victory** arc found in certain folk tales, horror, and late-act film structures.

| Stage | Psychological Function |
|-------|------------------------|
| **Ordinary World** | Establishes the lie the protagonist lives with. The comfort is already cracked. |
| **Inciting Incident** | Something uninvited enters. The pattern breaks. The protagonist’s first reaction reveals character more than the event itself. |
| **Rising Action** | Want vs. Need is tested. Alliances and obstacles begin to swap places. Recurring symbols start to “want” something. |
| **First Climax (False Victory)** | A real but incomplete victory. The protagonist believes they have learned something. The reader (and often the writer) is allowed a moment of relief. |
| **The Fall** | The cost of that victory arrives. The fall must feel earned by both external pressure *and* the protagonist’s own flaw, otherwise it becomes melodrama or moral lesson. |
| **Dark Night** | The deepest self-belief is confronted. This is the stage where the protagonist must change or the second climax cannot belong to them. |
| **Second Climax (True Climax)** | The harder thing the protagonist could not do at the first climax. Requires real sacrifice. The recurring symbol is transformed rather than merely repeated. |
| **Falling Action** | Aftermath. What remains? What is missing from the room? The new ordinary begins. |
| **Resolution** | Final image and final thought. The engine is suspicious of pure happiness or pure sadness; both can be lies. |

The psychology of the double climax is explicit in the code and README:

- The first climax must be a *real* victory in miniature, otherwise the fall feels arbitrary.
- The second climax must be a *different kind* of action, otherwise it is just a louder version of the first.
- The fall between them is what gives the second climax its weight. Without the fall, the second climax collapses into repetition.

### 3. The Symbolic Palette as Psychological Instrument

Four parallel registers run underneath every decision:

- **Colors** — emotional and archetypal temperature (black = void/potential, gold = achievement or false prize, indigo = the veil thinning, etc.)
- **Lights** — quality of consciousness and revelation (candlelight = fragile hope, fluorescent = institutional exposure, total dark = surrender before rebirth)
- **Thoughts** — internal states (doubt, resolve, shame, longing, clarity…)
- **Symbols** — concrete images that carry traditional Western esoteric weight (door, mirror, key, wound, threshold, serpent, garden…)

These are drawn from color symbolism, elemental associations, alchemy, and tarot, but the engine treats them as **starting points**, not rigid correspondences. Their purpose is to give the writer a second language in which to think about the same decision.

When the writer records a decision (`decide`), the engine performs deterministic keyword extraction and immediately reflects the meanings back. This creates a feedback loop: the writer sees the symbolic charge of their own words and is invited to deepen or correct it.

### 4. How the Prompts Work Psychologically

Each stage has three layers of intervention:

1. **Ask** — open questions that force the writer to look at belief, perception, and sensory atmosphere rather than pure plot logistics.
2. **Warn** — explicit failure modes for that stage (e.g., “If the ordinary world is too pleasant, the fall will not land”).
3. **Suggest** — concrete craft moves that keep the symbolic and structural registers in conversation (recurring colors, light sources that will later be lost, symbols that return transformed).

The questions are designed to surface:

- Self-deception (“What does the protagonist believe about themselves that is not true?”)
- Unconscious ritual and avoidance
- The difference between what is won and what is understood
- The quality of light and color as proxies for psychological state

### 5. What the Engine Deliberately Avoids

- It does not generate scenes or dialogue.
- It does not score “good” or “bad” writing.
- It does not impose a single mythic template (Hero’s Journey, Save the Cat, etc.) as dogma.
- It does not claim the symbolic meanings are universal truths; they are provocations.

Its stance is closer to a rigorous, slightly severe muse: it keeps the writer honest about both the structural spine and the symbolic undercurrent, and it refuses to do the actual writing.

### 6. Underlying Theory of Mind for Writers

Muse Engine assumes that many writers (especially those working in a “symbolic sub-layer” tradition) already carry an intuitive second register while drafting. The tool externalizes and systematizes that practice:

- Structure prevents the story from wandering.
- Palette prevents the story from becoming flat.
- The dialogue between the two keeps the writer from lying to themselves about either the plot or its meaning.

In short: **Muse Engine is less a writing assistant and more a structured mirror for the writer’s own narrative unconscious.** It records decisions, extracts their symbolic charge, situates them on a psychologically weighted pyramid, and then asks the questions the writer has not yet asked.
